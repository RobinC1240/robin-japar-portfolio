Designing a GenAI Marketing Operating System

A growing B2B technology company needed to scale its marketing capability with a junior team and little budget for specialized tools.

I designed a GenAI marketing operating system that turned senior marketing judgment into a repeatable workflow the team could use across research, strategy, creative development, production, and review.

The challenge

Access to AI wasn’t the same as knowing what good looked like.

The team could generate content quickly. The harder part was deciding what should be created, which sources were credible, which claims were supportable, how product value should be framed, and whether the final work was ready to publish.

Those decisions were still happening in the heads of experienced reviewers. Every new assignment created another point of dependency.

I needed a way to make that judgment visible, reusable, and easier for the team to apply.

The operating system

I built the workflow around the decisions experienced marketers make.

I broke the work into stages based on the decisions an experienced marketer would normally make along the way.

Research established what was true. Strategy defined what mattered. Copy shaped the message. Creative direction translated the message into a visual idea. Production built the asset. QA checked the work against the decisions that came before it.

Each stage produced the working context for the next, so the reasoning traveled with the work as it moved across tools and people.

One workflow in practice

A single social post became the first full test of the system.

Static Social gave me a contained way to test the model end to end.

One post moved through nine defined stages, from research and content strategy through production, AI QA, creative approval, and executive approval. Each stage had a required deliverable, a clear owner, and a defined handoff.

The workflow gave the team one repeatable path from assignment to approved asset.

Encoding expertise

I translated brand standards into AI-ready Markdown.

The workflow became reusable when the guidance inside each stage became reusable too.

I built prompts that defined the role, objective, required inputs, source rules, constraints, output structure, and quality criteria for each task. I also converted brand standards, writing guidance, and product context into machine-readable Markdown files that could travel with the workflow.

This gave the team a consistent operating context across tools and assignments.

Governance

Scale required rules the team could trust.

As the workflow became more reusable, I added controls around the places where AI introduced risk.

Claims had to trace back to approved sources. Confidential information stayed out of free AI accounts. Approved strategy and copy became locked inputs. Generated visuals could support a concept without fabricating product functionality. AI QA could flag issues and recommend corrections. Human reviewers retained final approval.

These rules made governance part of everyday execution.

Team enablement

The playbooks gave the team a shared way to work.

The team could see what came into each stage, what had already been decided, what they were responsible for producing, and what quality looked like before the work moved forward.

The playbooks created a shared operating language across research, strategy, copy, creative development, production, and review.

Junior marketers gained a clearer path through unfamiliar work, while senior reviewers could spend more time on the decisions that required deeper experience.

Scaling the system

One workflow became the foundation for a broader marketing system.

The Static Social workflow established a reusable pattern the team could apply to other deliverables.

The same operating model expanded across product teasers, demos, one-pagers, blog content, video scripts, thought leadership, case studies, SEO updates, email campaigns, and sales materials.

Each workflow adapted to the work while preserving the same core structure: clear inputs, reusable prompts, controlled handoffs, quality gates, and human review.

What began as one repeatable process became a growing library of marketing playbooks built from the same operating model.
